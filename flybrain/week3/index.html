Can the fly talk to a language model?

Week 3 rebuilds the rest of the "fly brain to ChatGPT" demo on the calibrated male brain. Words play into the simulated fly, a readout turns its spikes back into words, a language model reads them and picks an action, and (from Day 3) that action is fed back into the fly. This page grows as each day finishes.

What Day 6 found

The loop is now fast enough. The simulator can save its state and resume it, so each round continues the same brain instead of replaying from the start. A 3-round loop now takes 5–7.5 minutes instead of about 20, which meets the 10-minute goal. Resumed runs are spike-for-spike identical to one long run, and the results match Day 5 exactly.

A real language model is now in the loop. Claude (claude-sonnet-5-5) answers each round live, through the Claude Code command line, and its choice is played straight back into the brain. Its choices: real wiring seed 4000 turn left, go forward, eat (the same as the old stand-in); seed 4001 stop, eat, eat; seed 4002 turn left, turn left, eat; shuffled wiring turn left, turn left, eat. It disagreed with the old rule-based stand-in in 4 of 12 rounds. Claude is not deterministic: the identical round-1 sentence got "turn left" three times and "stop" once. A different reply also changes the brain going into the next round, and every run still decoded all 15 words and read back all 3 actions.

The shuffled brain passes too. All 4 runs, including the one with shuffled wiring, decode 15 of 15 words and read back 3 of 3 actions. The shuffled brain does it with lower confidence. So the loop shows that the readout pipeline works, but not, on its own, that the real wiring is needed.

Still labels. The action codes are arbitrary patterns on the hearing neurons, not natural steering commands. Day 7 is the write-up.

days 3–5 · 10–11 nov 2026

The loop closes: the fly's action goes back into the fly

Each run is three rounds in one continuous simulation (10.5 s of brain time) of the calibrated MaleCNS brain. In each round, five words play in, the readout decodes them, and the language model picks an action. The action is then played back into the brain as a pattern on its hearing neurons, and a second readout reads it off the descending neurons, the brain's outputs to the body. The next round starts in the same brain, with the earlier rounds' activity still present.

What Days 3–5 found

The loop works end to end. Over 4 runs of 3 rounds, all 60 words are decoded and all 12 actions are read back correctly from the descending neurons, inside one continuous simulation. When the runner replays the simulation from the start at each step, the earlier spikes come out identical every time (8 of 8 checks).

"Turn left" is a label, not a steering command. The codes are carried mainly by the giant fiber (DNp01) and a few other descending neurons (DNg33, DNp12, DNp11). The steering neurons DNa01–DNa03 stay at or below about 3 Hz for every code. So the fly's brain reliably carries which code it got, but it does not turn.

The right ear was missing from the wiring map. In MaleCNS the right JO neurons make 34% of the left side's output synapses, and only about 5% for JO-A. Many right JO neurons have no synapses at all. FlyWire, a different fly, is balanced, so this looks like an incomplete reconstruction, not biology. Before the fix, right-side codes reached no descending neuron at all. An optional mirror calibration gives each right JO neuron its left partner's outputs, and that is what makes "turn right" work.

A shuffled brain closes the loop too. A brain with shuffled wiring still reads back 36 of 37 held-out actions and all 12 loop actions, with lower confidence (0.81–0.93 against mostly 0.97 or more). Reading back a pattern we injected ourselves works with almost any wiring. What the real wiring changes is reach: the codes drive 445 descending neurons in the real brain and 24 in the shuffled one.

Still open after Day 5. The replies are still the offline rule-based stand-in, not a real language model. A run took 18–23 minutes of computing, against a goal of about 10, because the simulator could not save and resume its state. Day 6 fixed both: runs take 5–7.5 minutes, and Claude now gives the replies.

What Day 2 found

The fly → LLM half works. The demo episode from the plan (vinegar, ripe fruit, sugar, warm, bright → vinegar · fruit · sugar · hot · looming) decodes 50 of 50 words over 10 noise seeds, with every top probability at least 0.998. Six short scenarios decode 12 of 12. The readout was trained once, on isolated words, and needs no retraining for episodes on this brain model.

The logged replies are placeholders. The project has no API key yet, so the logged replies come from an offline rule-based stand-in (danger → turn away or stop, food → eat, attractive smells → go forward). It is there for testing and says nothing about the fly. Only the decoding is a result. With ✦ Ask a real LLM you can send the same prompts to Claude or GPT with your own key and see what a real model picks.

Your own words are replayed, not simulated. The site has no server, and one live run takes about 1.5 minutes of computing, so Tell the fly what it senses plays back recorded responses instead: 262 word windows from the Day 1 episodes and the Day 2 runs, every one decoded correctly. Your text is first matched to the fly's 17 words, by a real LLM with your key or by the project's keyword table without one.

Carried into Day 3. Looming leaves 400–1,200 neurons firing after the episode, so the action code will start with that state present. The action readout has to be trained with that history.

What Day 1 found

It mostly works back to back. A readout trained only on single words still decodes 95.5% of windows with no gap between words, above the 95% bar in the plan, so the loop uses 0 ms gaps. The first word of every episode is always right.

The errors were a lasting cold/humid state, not the previous word. All 9 errors were words read as cold, mostly hot → cold. Every one came after cold or humid earlier in the episode, often two or three words back. About 270 neurons kept firing for seconds and pushed the thermosensory pathway towards cold. Gaps of 250 or 500 ms did not help.

Fix: brain → sensory synapses as presynaptic inhibition. In the fly, central input onto sensory terminals turns down their transmitter release; it does not make them spike. Modelling it that way (GABA-A and GABA-B traces, one constant K = 0.05 fitted to keep FlyWire sugar → MN9) removes the state: 0 errors with all neurons, and the DN + MN readout rises from 82.5% to 90%.

Still open. Wind and looming leave about 450 neurons firing through a loop outside the antennal lobe. It does not confuse any word. Day 2 sends the decoded words to an LLM; Days 3–4 send its chosen action back into the fly.
